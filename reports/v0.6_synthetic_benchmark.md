# v0.6 — Synthetic benchmark

## Objective

Compare ESD PELT vs same-model window-aggregation on planted
events. **POST_HOC:** primary grid used **10** replicates/cell
(plan said 50; 50-rep run was ~80 min and was stopped).

## Methods

144 cells × 10 reps: width × Δ ∈ {0.5,1.5,3} × noise ∈
{0.05,0.2,0.5} × GAIN/LOSS. Bin 200 bp. Penalty 8, min_bins=3,
filter=none, FDR 0.05. Spacing grid: gaps 0/400/2000/10000 bp.

## Parameters

See `results/v0.6/synthetic_design.tsv`. Seed `20260+cell*1000+rep`.

## Results

FACT (median over 2,880 method-rows):

| method | F1 | dir acc | recall | precision | one-call | med bound err |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ESD | 0.757 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.631 | 1.00 | 800 bp |
| window | 0.980 | 1.00 | 0.98 | 1.000 | 1.00 | 200 bp |

ESD F1 by planted width: 200 bp **0.00**, 500 **0.02**, 1 kb
**0.24**, 2 kb **0.43**, 5 kb **0.74**, 10 kb **0.71**, 25 kb
**0.80**, 50 kb **0.88**.

FACT: 200 bp events cannot be emitted (`min_bins=3` → 600 bp
floor). That is a design constraint, not a scoring bug.

FACT: same-direction gap=0: ESD mean merge count 0.9; window 0.3.
Opposite-direction neighbors: ESD merge 0; direction accuracy 1
for both methods.

## Uncertainty

INFERENCE: window-aggregation wins F1/precision/boundary on these
block-constant toys. ESD becomes competitive only as width grows.
That supports H2 (broad) more than H1 (general boundary).

## Failures

nrep=10 is POST_HOC. Gradual/peak-geometry/background secondary
cells were **not** fully enumerated as separate tables (spacing +
primary cover the main H3/H4/H6 questions).

## Interpretation

Gates B (direction): **PASS**.
Gate C (boundary advantage): **not** generally supported vs
windows; **partial** for wide events.
Gate D: opposite events not systematically merged.

## Decision

Direction OK. Domain advantage is width-dependent.

## Next action

Do not claim overall synthetic superiority.
