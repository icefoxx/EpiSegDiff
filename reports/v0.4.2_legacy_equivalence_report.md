# v0.4.2 — Legacy equivalence

## Objective

Show that the new chromosome-wise dispatcher does not change
segmentation on domains where the v0.4.1 implementation already
finishes.

## Methods

`segment_genome(..., engine="pelt")` calls `segment_chromosome` →
`segment_track` → the same `pelt_gaussian_cpp` used in v0.4.1, with
only the candidate-set storage changed from per-step `std::vector`
reallocation to pre-sized arrays. Cost, penalty, `min_width`, and
backtracking are unchanged.

Compared:

- synthetic series in `tests/testthat/test-v042-scalability.R`
  (PELT vs OP, PELT vs FPOP on clean steps, two-chromosome isolation)
- real GSE280947 z-score prefix: chr1 first 20,000 bins

## Parameters

penalty 24, `min_bins = 3`, Gaussian PELT, unfiltered z-score.

## Results

FACT: on chr1 prefix 20k, legacy `segment_track` and
`segment_genome` both emit **12** segments with **identical**
`start`/`end`.

FACT: unit tests require PELT and OP to agree on a small step
series, and PELT/FPOP to agree on clean synthetic series.

FACT: a change point cannot cross chromosomes (`segment_chromosome`
rejects multi-chrom input; `segment_genome` splits first).

FACT: genuine NA/invalid gaps produce two CVRs and no interval that
covers the gap.

INFERENCE: on any single chromosome the dispatcher is the legacy
algorithm. Combining chromosomes is a `rbind` in deterministic
chrom order.

## Uncertainty

A first-cut `fpop_gaussian_cpp` does **not** match PELT cost on
noisy real z-scores and is **not** used for the official
`filter=none` run.

## Failures

None on the required PELT path.

## Interpretation

Gate A (legacy PELT semantics): **PASS**.

## Limitations

Equivalence is for PELT, not for the experimental FPOP routine.

## Decision

Proceed to the full `filter=none` run with `engine=pelt`.
